I read this while I was still living in Shanghai, where the book is set. Ballard grew up there and was interned as a boy not far from neighbourhoods I walked through after work. That overlap was stranger than I expected — fiction and streets at the same time.

Jim's war is partly spectacle and weather, which some readers find cold; it felt honest to me. And the book sat in my head alongside something you sometimes hear living in China — how competitive or bitter people can be about America, in ways that sit oddly next to the history of the Pacific war and what the US did in that conflict. The novel doesn't lecture you on any of that. It just leaves you holding both pictures at once.

Glad I read it here rather than from a distance.
