It's interesting to see Britain from an immigrant's perspective. Z's diary is written in an English that slowly improves, and along the way she spots all the odd inconsistencies in the language and the culture that natives have stopped noticing.

The shoe scene stayed with me. She tells her language teacher that her parents might have made the teacher's shoes back in China — said innocently, and met with embarrassment and something close to disgust. There's a lot of snobbery in Britain, sadly. Much of it is inherited rather than chosen, so I can't blame individuals too much, especially older generations who grew up inside it. Still, I winced.

I liked Z. Some of the romance dragged. I finished the book fond of her and slightly sheepish about home.
