Crazy book. I read it on the Shanghai metro, and the murder scene is genuinely shocking — clumsy, panicked, sickening, not cinematic at all. I had to put it down once.

Raskolnikov talks himself into being a Napoleon who can kill for the greater good, then unravels in fevers and guilt. I'm not sure the book works as the takedown of utilitarianism Dostoevsky supposedly intended — a committed utilitarian could just say he picked the wrong case. But it made me think about morality, and the value of shared moral codes, more than any philosophy seminar I've sat through.

Heavy, and slow to leave my head. Glad I read it once.
