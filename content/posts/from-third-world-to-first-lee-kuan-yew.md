Long book. Lee's second volume of memoirs — Singapore kicked out of Malaysia in 1965, then the climb to absurd prosperity. I read it in chunks after work.

The pragmatism is what I remember: borrow what works, drop what doesn't, no patience for ideology. Some of his lines about welfare and the press made me uncomfortable; he's open that they traded liberty for order, which at least saves you from fairy-tale thinking.

Living in Shanghai, bits felt familiar — bureaucracy, exams, infrastructure obsession — like reading someone's diary from a parallel universe. Occasionally self-satisfied, never boring for me.
