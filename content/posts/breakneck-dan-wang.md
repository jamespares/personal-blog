I read much of this on the high-speed train to Beijing — cranes out of the window the whole way. Dan Wang's contrast between an engineering-led China and a lawyer-led West felt plausible sitting on that track. Whether it's the whole story, I don't know. But it matched what I've seen of the difference between process and building.

Some observations stuck hard, especially the chapters on railways and cement — the sheer scale of pouring and laying reset how I think about the word "infrastructure". And Wang is no cheerleader. When he writes about applying engineering logic to people, I believed him more because he'd already convinced me on the bridges.

A very interesting book for anyone trying to understand why things here look the way they do.
