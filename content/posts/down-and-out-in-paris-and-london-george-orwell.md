I enjoyed this. The Paris kitchen chapters are hot, exact and grimly funny, and the passage where he finally eats properly after days of hunger made me get up and make toast.

Something troubled me underneath, though. There is a romantic glow around poverty and destitution that I don't trust. Orwell could leave; the tramps couldn't. I'm not sure the book really helps the British intellectual tradition on that score — at times it reads as poverty-as-purification for a sensitive observer who always had a door out.

Interesting nonetheless. I'm still of two minds about it, and I probably will be if I reread it.
