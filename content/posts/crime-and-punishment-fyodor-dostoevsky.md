I read this on the Shanghai metro over two weeks. Probably the wrong place. The axe murder is horrible in the best way — panicked, ugly, not cinematic. I had to put the book down once.

Raskolnikov talks himself into being a Napoleon who can kill for the greater good, then falls apart in fevers and paranoia long before Siberia. I cared about that unraveling more than the philosophy debate. Whether utilitarianism "loses" logically didn't keep me up at night; whether this one man could live with himself did.

Took me a fortnight to finish and longer to stop thinking about it. Heavy, but I don't regret picking it up.
