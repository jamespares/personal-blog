Interesting to see Britain from an immigrant's perspective. Z's diary is written in an English that slowly improves, and along the way she notices all the logical inconsistencies in the language and the culture that natives have stopped seeing.

The shoe scene stuck with me. She tells her language teacher that her parents might have made the teacher's shoes back in China — said innocently, and met with embarrassment and something like disgust. There is a lot of snobbery in Britain, sadly. Much of it is socially constructed, so I can't blame individuals too much, especially older generations who grew up inside it. Still, I winced.

I liked Z. Some of the romance dragged. I finished feeling fond of her and slightly sheepish about home.
